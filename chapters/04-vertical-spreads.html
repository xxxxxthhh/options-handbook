Chapter 04 · Vertical Spreads

垂直价差：把观点做成有限风险的结构Vertical Spreads: Turning a View into Defined Risk

单腿是一个观点。价差是一个观点加一个边界。加上第二条腿，你放弃了尾部的收益，换来三样东西：更低的成本、更小的波动率敏感度，以及一个在下单之前就已经知道的最坏结果。 A single leg is a view. A spread is a view plus a boundary. Adding a second leg forfeits the tail, and buys three things in return: lower cost, lower sensitivity to volatility, and a worst case you already know before you click the button.

4.1 为什么要加第二条腿4.1 Why a Second Leg

第 2 章的 Long Call 有一个隐藏的假设：你为全部上涨空间付了钱。股价涨到 $200、$500、$1000 的可能性都被打包进你付出的 Premium 里——哪怕你自己都不相信它能涨到那儿。在 IV 高企的时候，这部分"你并不相信的尾部"可能占了权利金的一大半。 The long call in Chapter 2 carries a hidden assumption: you paid for all of the upside. The chance of the stock reaching $200, $500, $1,000 is bundled into the premium you handed over — even the part you don't believe in yourself. When IV is elevated, that unbelieved tail can be most of what you're paying for.

Vertical Spread（垂直价差）的做法很直接：把你不相信的那段卖掉。同一个标的、同一个到期日、方向相反的两条腿，只有 Strike 不同——这就是"垂直"的含义（在期权链上，同一到期日的合约纵向排列）。 A vertical spread does something blunt: it sells the part you don't believe in. Two legs on the same underlying, same expiration, opposite directions, differing only in strike — which is what "vertical" means (on an option chain, one expiry's contracts run down the page).

第二条腿带来三个变化，值得逐个记住： The second leg changes three things, each worth committing to memory:

① 成本下降。卖出的那条腿收回一部分权利金，净支出（Net Debit，净借记）通常只有裸买的 50%–70%。成本下降直接把盈亏平衡点拉近，也把"完全归零"的金额变小。② Vega 下降。你同时买入和卖出了波动率，两条腿的 Vega 大部分互相抵消——这意味着IV Crush 对你的伤害小得多。③ 收益封顶。这是代价：无论股价涨到哪里，你的收益都停在"宽度 − 净借记"。 ① Cost falls. The short leg refunds part of the premium; the net debit is typically only 50–70% of the outright purchase. Lower cost pulls the breakeven closer and shrinks the amount that can go to zero. ② Vega falls. You are long and short volatility at once, so the two legs' vega largely cancels — meaning IV crush hurts you far less. ③ Upside is capped. That's the price: however far the stock runs, your gain stops at "width minus net debit."

价差的天花板不是免费的，但它疼得比多数人想象的晚。沿用上面的示意结构：到期股价 $50 时，裸 Call 回报约 +436%，价差封顶在 +400%——几乎打平，因为价差的成本本来就低。真正拉开差距要等到 $70：裸 Call 约 +1150%，价差仍然是 +400%。结论是：在温和到中等的行情里价差几乎总是更优，只有在你没预料到的爆发式行情里才明显吃亏。所以选择价差的真正问题不是"我会不会少赚"，而是"我的判断里有没有爆发式行情这一项"。 The ceiling isn't free, but it bites later than most people assume. Using the same illustrative structure: at $50 the outright call returns about +436% and the spread caps at +400% — effectively a tie, because the spread cost less to begin with. The gap only opens at $70: the outright returns roughly +1150% while the spread is still +400%. So spreads win in mild-to-moderate moves and only clearly lose in the explosive move you didn't foresee. The real question isn't "will I leave money on the table," it's "does my thesis actually contain an explosive move?"

思考Think 如果 2020 年 11 月 6 日你的判断是"疫苗一旦成功，DAL 会一路回到疫情前的 $60"，价差还是正确的结构吗？换个问法：你的目标价，是从基本面推导出来的，还是从"我希望赚多少"倒推出来的？这两者会指向完全不同的 Strike。 If on November 6, 2020 your view had been "once a vaccine works, DAL goes all the way back to its pre-pandemic $60," would the spread still be the right structure? Put differently: is your target price derived from the fundamentals, or reverse-engineered from how much you'd like to make? Those two roads lead to very different strikes.

Bear Put Spread 是 Bull Call Spread 的镜像，但它最常见的身份不是投机，而是预算受限的保险。第 2 章的 Protective Put 有一个现实问题：真正需要保险的时候（市场紧张、IV 高企），保费也最贵。卖掉一个更低的 Strike，等于对保险公司说："我只要覆盖前 20% 的跌幅，再往下的世界末日情形我自己扛。"保费因此可以砍到三分之一左右。 The bear put spread mirrors the bull call spread, but its most common role isn't speculation — it's insurance on a budget. Chapter 2's protective put has a practical problem: exactly when you want coverage (nervous market, elevated IV), coverage is most expensive. Selling a lower strike tells the insurer: "cover me for the first 20% down; below that, the end-of-the-world scenario is mine to carry." That can cut the bill to roughly a third.

适用场景：① 你想对冲，但认为最可能的情形是"回调 15%–25%"而不是"崩盘 50%"；② IV 已经很高，全额保险的成本会吃掉太多组合收益；③ 你有一个明确的下方支撑位判断，卖出的 Strike 就放在那里。 When to use it: ① You want a hedge but believe the likely case is a 15–25% pullback, not a 50% collapse; ② IV is already high and full coverage would eat too much of the portfolio's return; ③ you have a specific view of downside support, and the short strike goes there.

把 4.3 的两条腿方向对调：卖出 $100 的 Put 收 $4，买入 $90 的 Put 付 $1.50，净收到 $2.50。这叫 Credit Spread（贷记价差）。它表达的观点不是"我看涨"，而是更弱的一句话——"我认为它不会跌破 $100"。股价横盘、小涨、甚至小跌到 $100 以上，你都赢。 Flip the two legs of §4.3: sell the $100 put for $4, buy the $90 put for $1.50, and you receive $2.50 net. This is a credit spread. Its view isn't "I'm bullish" but something weaker — "I don't think it breaks $100." Sideways, up a little, even down a little while staying above $100: you win.

这是本书第一次出现卖方的定义风险版本。第 2 章的 CSP 需要锁定 $10,000 现金去接货；同样的 $100 Put，配上一条 $90 的保护腿，最大亏损锁死在 $750，占用的资金也降到那个数量级。Defined Risk（定义风险）不是修辞——那条买入的腿是一张真实的合约，在最坏的日子里替你封住了下方。 This is the book's first defined-risk version of a short. Chapter 2's CSP requires $10,000 of cash set aside to take delivery; the same $100 put paired with a $90 protective leg caps the worst case at $750, and the capital tied up drops to that order of magnitude. Defined risk isn't rhetoric — the long leg is a real contract that closes the floor on the worst day.

胜率幻觉：80% 的胜率不等于赚钱The win-rate illusion: 80% right ≠ profitable

上面那个价差：赢的时候赚 $2.50，输的时候亏 $7.50。要让期望值为零，胜率必须达到 75%（7.5 ÷ 10）。而市场对这张 Put 的定价，正是让它大约落在这个胜率附近——Delta 已经把概率写在标签上了。 That spread makes $2.50 when it wins and loses $7.50 when it doesn't. Break-even expectancy requires a 75% win rate (7.5 ÷ 10). And the market's pricing of that put puts it right around there — delta has already written the probability on the label.

所以"我的贷记价差 80% 都赢了"本身不是任何信息。真正的问题是：那 20% 输的时候，你是按规则认赔 $7.50，还是把它 Roll 成一个更大的头寸继续赌？贷记价差的账户曲线之所以经常呈现"缓慢上升 + 悬崖式下跌"，原因几乎从来不是胜率不够，而是输的那几次没有按结构原本的最大亏损收场。 So "my credit spreads win 80% of the time" carries no information by itself. The real question is: on the 20% that lose, do you take the $7.50 by the rules — or roll it into a bigger position and keep gambling? The reason credit-spread equity curves so often look like a slow climb followed by a cliff is almost never an inadequate win rate; it's that the losses weren't allowed to end at the structure's designed maximum.

思考Think 同一个"我认为它不会跌破 $100"的观点，可以用 Bull Put Spread（收 $2.50，最大亏 $7.50）表达，也可以用 Bull Call Spread（付 $3，最大赚 $7）表达。两者的盈亏图形状几乎完全一样。那么它们的区别到底在哪里？提示：想想 Theta 的方向、账户里占用的资金，以及被提前指派的可能性。 The same view — "it won't break $100" — can be expressed as a bull put spread (collect $2.50, risk $7.50) or a bull call spread (pay $3, make up to $7). Their payoff diagrams are nearly identical. So where is the real difference? Hint: consider the sign of theta, the capital the account must reserve, and the possibility of early assignment.

4.5 宽度与 Strike：概率和赔率的跷跷板4.5 Width and Strike: The Probability-Payoff Seesaw

价差只有两个自由度：放在哪里（Strike 的位置）和拉多宽（Width）。这两个旋钮控制的是同一件事的两端——概率和赔率——而且它们永远反向移动。市场不会送你既高概率又高赔率的结构；如果你觉得找到了，通常是漏算了某个风险。 A spread has only two degrees of freedom: where you put it (strike placement) and how wide you make it (width). Those two knobs control the two ends of one thing — probability and payoff — and they always move in opposite directions. The market does not hand out structures that are both high-probability and high-payoff; if you think you found one, you've usually failed to price some risk.

一个实用的定位工具是 Delta。0.30 Delta 的短腿，粗略意味着约 30% 的概率在到期时变成 ITM。多数系统化的贷记价差做法把短腿放在 0.15–0.30 Delta 之间——不是因为这个区间有魔力，而是因为再往外走，收到的权利金会小到无法覆盖点差和偶尔的大亏。宽度则主要由资金决定：$5 宽的价差最大亏损 $500 减去权利金，$10 宽的就是两倍，这直接决定了你一次能开几张。 A practical placement tool is delta. A 0.30-delta short leg roughly means a 30% chance of finishing ITM. Most systematic credit-spread programs place the short leg between 0.15 and 0.30 delta — not because that band is magic, but because further out the premium shrinks below what can cover spreads and the occasional full loss. Width is mostly a capital decision: a $5-wide spread risks $500 minus the credit, a $10-wide one risks twice that, and that arithmetic decides how many you can hold at once.

与实盘的连接Bridge to live trading

三件在纸上看不见、在实盘每天遇到的事。① 双边点差是两倍的。价差有两条腿，你要穿过两次买卖价差；流动性差的标的上，这项摩擦可以吃掉全部理论优势——只在期权链密集、点差是几分钱的标的上做价差。② 用组合单，不要分腿下。券商的 spread order 会把两条腿作为一个整体成交；分两次下单意味着中间那几秒你是裸露的。③ 短腿可能被提前指派。美式期权的 Short Put 在深度 ITM 或除息日前可能被提前指派，你会突然持有 100 股正股；这不改变最大亏损（长腿还在），但会占用资金并制造一天的方向暴露。 Three things invisible on paper and unavoidable in a live account. ① You cross the spread twice. Two legs means two bid-ask crossings; on illiquid names that friction can consume the entire theoretical edge — only trade spreads where the chain is dense and quotes are pennies wide. ② Use a combo order, never leg in. A broker's spread order fills both legs as one unit; entering them separately leaves you naked for those few seconds. ③ The short leg can be assigned early. An American-style short put deep ITM, or one facing an ex-dividend date, may be assigned early and leave you suddenly holding 100 shares. That doesn't change your maximum loss (the long leg is still there), but it does tie up capital and create a day of directional exposure.

买入 $50 Call 付 $4，卖出 $60 Call 收 $1.50。这个价差的最大盈利是？You buy the $50 call for $4 and sell the $60 call for $1.50. The maximum profit of this spread is:

C。宽度 $10 是两条腿之间的总空间，但你为进入这个结构付了 $2.50，所以净收益上限是 $7.50。$2.50 是你的最大亏损（B 描述的是亏损而非盈利）；"无限"属于没有上腿的裸买 Call。 C. The $10 width is the total space between the legs, but you paid $2.50 to enter, so the net cap is $7.50. The $2.50 is your maximum loss (B describes the loss, not the gain); "unlimited" belongs to an outright call with no upper leg.

在 IV 处于历史高位时，价差相对裸买期权的最大优势是什么？When IV is at historic highs, the biggest advantage of a spread over an outright option is:

B。高 IV 让你买的腿变贵，但同时也让你卖的腿变贵——两者大部分抵消，净 Vega 被压得很低，IV Crush 的伤害也随之变小。A 混淆了结构与胜率（同样 Strike 的价差胜率并不天然更高）；C 错在价差只是净 Theta 更小，两条腿都仍在衰减；D 忽略了平仓同样要穿过两条腿的点差。 B. High IV makes the leg you buy expensive — and the leg you sell expensive too. They largely cancel, net vega collapses, and IV crush does far less damage. A confuses structure with probability (a spread at the same strike has no inherently better win rate); C is wrong because a spread only has smaller net theta — both legs still decay; D ignores that closing also means crossing two bid-ask spreads.

一个宽度 $10、收到 $2 权利金的贷记价差，长期不亏钱所需的最低胜率约是？A $10-wide credit spread collecting $2 needs roughly what minimum win rate to break even over time?

D。赢一次 +$2，输一次 −$8（宽度 $10 减去权利金 $2）。设胜率 p，期望值为零需要 2p = 8(1−p)，解得 p = 0.8。这正是"高胜率策略"的真相：胜率高是被赔率买来的，不是免费的优势。而市场对短腿的定价，本来就让它大致落在这个胜率附近。 D. A win is +$2, a loss is −$8 ($10 width less the $2 credit). With win rate p, zero expectancy requires 2p = 8(1−p), so p = 0.8. This is the truth about "high-probability strategies": the high win rate was purchased with bad payoff odds, it is not free edge. And the market's pricing of the short leg puts it right around there to begin with.

你确信某只股票会崩盘，可能跌 50% 以上。下面哪个结构最不适合表达这个观点？You're convinced a stock will crash, possibly more than 50%. Which structure is least suited to that view?

A。价差的整个设计前提是"跌幅有限"——下腿把收益封死在宽度以内。你越确信是极端行情，封顶的代价就越大。这正是 ARKK 案例的教训：-43% 的跌幅击穿地板后，裸 Put 的表现明显更好。B/C/D 都保留了尾部收益，只是在成本和时间上做取舍。 A. A spread is built on the premise that the move is bounded — the lower leg seals the payoff at the width. The more convinced you are of an extreme move, the more that ceiling costs. This is exactly the ARKK lesson: once the -43% blew through the floor, the outright put clearly won. B, C and D all retain tail participation and merely trade off cost against time.

你的 Bull Put Spread 中，卖出的那条 Put 在除息日前被提前指派。发生了什么？The short put in your bull put spread is assigned early, just before an ex-dividend date. What has happened?

B。提前指派让你按 Strike 买入 100 股，但你买入的那条低 Strike Put 完好无损，它仍然把股价下方封死——结构的最大亏损没有改变。变化的是资金占用和一天的方向暴露。常见的正确处理是：卖掉股票并同时平掉长腿，或者直接行权长腿把股票交出去。券商不会替你做这个决定（D 错）。 B. Early assignment means you bought 100 shares at the strike, but the lower-strike put you own is untouched and still seals the downside — the structure's maximum loss is unchanged. What changed is capital usage and a day of directional exposure. The usual correct response: sell the shares and close the long leg together, or exercise the long leg to deliver them. The broker will not make that decision for you (so not D).

本章要点Key Takeaways

垂直价差做的是一件事：用你不相信的那段行情，去补贴你相信的那段。借记价差把裸买期权的成本砍掉三到五成，代价是收益封顶；贷记价差把无抵押卖方的无限风险锁进一个矩形，代价是赢面虽大、赔率却差。两者的盈亏图可以几乎一模一样，区别在 Theta 的方向、占用的资金和被提前指派的可能——结构选择从来不只看形状。真正决定成败的仍然是那个老问题：你的判断里有没有极端行情这一项。相信有，就别封顶；相信没有，封顶反而让你在最可能发生的世界里赚得更多。至于"高胜率"，本章的算术已经说清楚了：胜率是用赔率买来的，市场从不打折出售确定性。 A vertical spread does one thing: it uses the part of the move you don't believe in to subsidize the part you do. Debit spreads cut an outright option's cost by a third to a half and cap the payoff in exchange. Credit spreads lock an uncollateralized short's unlimited risk inside a rectangle, in exchange for winning often but small. Their payoff diagrams can look identical; the differences live in the sign of theta, the capital reserved, and the chance of early assignment — structure is never chosen on shape alone. What decides the outcome is the old question: does your thesis contain an extreme move? If it does, don't cap it. If it doesn't, capping earns you more in the world most likely to happen. As for "high probability," the arithmetic in this chapter settles it: win rates are purchased with payoff odds, and the market never discounts certainty.
